The strange arithmetic: GF(256) calculator

Reed-Solomon works on bytes, but needs adding, multiplying and dividing bytes to always give a byte. In GF(256), adding is XOR, and multiplying adds logarithms (using a table built from repeatedly doubling and reducing by 0x11D). Try it:


      

    


    

      The generator polynomial for the current repair level
      

        
The repair bytes are chosen so the whole block is divisible by this polynomial (its roots are α0, α1, …). Coefficients are shown as powers of α (the number 2 in GF(256)).

      What this demo leaves out (honest limits)
      

        
    
          
  • Finding the code in a photo. A phone camera must locate the three corner squares, straighten the picture and turn pixels into a grid. Our decoder starts from the grid, because the repair step is the part we are explaining. (This is also why damaging a corner square would stop a real phone sooner than it stops this decoder.)
    • 
          
  • Versions 1–10 only (21×21 to 57×57 modules), and only the numeric, alphanumeric and byte modes. Kanji, structured-append and Micro QR are not included.
    • 
          
  • Real scanners can try extra tricks (for example “erasure decoding” when they know which bytes are unreadable), so a phone may read some codes this decoder gives up on, or the other way round.
